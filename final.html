Stokes' Theorem

Let SSS be an oriented, piecewise-smooth surface bounded by a simple closed curve C=∂SC = \partial SC=∂S. Let F⃗=⟨P,Q,R⟩\vec{F} = \langle P, Q, R \rangleF=⟨P,Q,R⟩ have continuous partial derivatives on an open region containing SSS. Then:

∮CF⃗⋅dr⃗=∬Scurl⁡(F⃗)⋅dS⃗\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S \operatorname{curl}(\vec{F})\cdot d\vec{S}∮C​F⋅dr=∬S​curl(F)⋅dS

Equivalently, using dS⃗=n^ dSd\vec{S} = \hat{n}\,dSdS=n^dS:

∮CF⃗⋅dr⃗=∬Scurl⁡(F⃗)⋅n^ dS\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S \operatorname{curl}(\vec{F})\cdot\hat{n}\,dS∮C​F⋅dr=∬S​curl(F)⋅n^dS

Orientation Convention

The boundary curve CCC is positively oriented relative to SSS when, walking along CCC with the surface on your left, n^\hat{n}n^ points away from your head. Equivalently: n^\hat{n}n^ and the direction of traversal of CCC satisfy the right-hand rule.

Curl Formula

For F⃗=⟨P,Q,R⟩\vec{F} = \langle P, Q, R \rangleF=⟨P,Q,R⟩:

curl⁡(F⃗)=∇×F⃗=∣i⃗j⃗k⃗∂x∂y∂zPQR∣=⟨Ry−Qz,  Pz−Rx,  Qx−Py⟩\operatorname{curl}(\vec{F}) = \nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix}\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ P & Q & R\end{vmatrix} = \langle R_y - Q_z,\; P_z - R_x,\; Q_x - P_y \ranglecurl(F)=∇×F=​i∂x​P​j​∂y​Q​k∂z​R​​=⟨Ry​−Qz​,Pz​−Rx​,Qx​−Py​⟩

Computing the Surface Integral Side

For surface z=g(x,y)z = g(x,y)z=g(x,y) with upward normal over region DDD:

∬Scurl⁡(F⃗)⋅dS⃗=∬D[−(Ry−Qz) gx−(Pz−Rx) gy+(Qx−Py)]dA\iint_S \operatorname{curl}(\vec{F})\cdot d\vec{S} = \iint_D \left[-(R_y-Q_z)\,g_x - (P_z-R_x)\,g_y + (Q_x-P_y)\right]dA∬S​curl(F)⋅dS=∬D​[−(Ry​−Qz​)gx​−(Pz​−Rx​)gy​+(Qx​−Py​)]dA

Key: Stokes' theorem converts a line integral into a surface integral (or vice versa). It generalizes Green's Theorem: when SSS is flat in the xyxyxy-plane, Stokes' theorem reduces exactly to Green's theorem.

Connection to Green's Theorem

Green's Theorem is the special case of Stokes' Theorem where SSS lies in the xyxyxy-plane (z=0z=0z=0, n^=k⃗\hat{n}=\vec{k}n^=k):

∮CP dx+Q dy=∬D(Qx−Py) dA\oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_D (Q_x - P_y)\,dA∮C​Pdx+Qdy=∬D​(Qx​−Py​)dA

Consequences

  • If curl⁡(F⃗)=0⃗\operatorname{curl}(\vec{F}) = \vec{0}curl(F)=0 (i.e., F⃗\vec{F}F is conservative), then ∮CF⃗⋅dr⃗=0\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r} = 0∮C​F⋅dr=0 for every closed curve CCC.
  • Two surfaces S1S_1S1​ and S2S_2S2​ with the same oriented boundary CCC give the same surface integral of curl⁡(F⃗)\operatorname{curl}(\vec{F})curl(F).
  • Stokes' theorem can simplify computation by replacing a hard surface with an easier one sharing the same boundary.

Divergence Theorem (Gauss's Theorem)

Let EEE be a simple solid region with outward-oriented boundary surface S=∂ES = \partial ES=∂E. Let F⃗=⟨P,Q,R⟩\vec{F} = \langle P, Q, R \rangleF=⟨P,Q,R⟩ have continuous partial derivatives on an open region containing EEE. Then:

∬SF⃗⋅dS⃗=∭Ediv⁡(F⃗) dV\iint_S \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{S} = \iiint_E \operatorname{div}(\vec{F})\,dV∬S​F⋅dS=∭E​div(F)dV

Divergence Formula

div⁡(F⃗)=∇⋅F⃗=∂P∂x+∂Q∂y+∂R∂z\operatorname{div}(\vec{F}) = \nabla\cdot\vec{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial R}{\partial z}div(F)=∇⋅F=∂x∂P​+∂y∂Q​+∂z∂R​

Orientation Convention

The boundary surface S=∂ES = \partial ES=∂E must be oriented with the outward normal (pointing away from the interior of EEE). If the surface is given with inward normal, multiply the result by −1-1−1.

Key: The Divergence Theorem converts a flux surface integral into a triple integral over the enclosed volume — often much easier to evaluate. It is especially useful when the surface consists of multiple pieces.

Physical Interpretation

The Divergence Theorem states that the net flux of F⃗\vec{F}F out of a closed surface equals the total divergence (source/sink density) integrated over the enclosed volume. If div⁡(F⃗)=0\operatorname{div}(\vec{F}) = 0div(F)=0 everywhere in EEE, then the net flux through ∂E\partial E∂E is zero.

Strategy: Replacing a Hard Surface

If SSS is an open surface (not closed), close it by adding a cap ScapS_{\text{cap}}Scap​, apply the Divergence Theorem to the closed surface S∪ScapS \cup S_{\text{cap}}S∪Scap​, then subtract the cap's contribution:

∬SF⃗⋅dS⃗=∭Ediv⁡(F⃗) dV−∬ScapF⃗⋅dS⃗\iint_S \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{S} = \iiint_E \operatorname{div}(\vec{F})\,dV - \iint_{S_{\text{cap}}} \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{S}∬S​F⋅dS=∭E​div(F)dV−∬Scap​​F⋅dS

Key Identities

  • div⁡(curl⁡(F⃗))=0\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{curl}(\vec{F})) = 0div(curl(F))=0 for any smooth F⃗\vec{F}F  — so ∬Scurl⁡(F⃗)⋅dS⃗=0\iint_S \operatorname{curl}(\vec{F})\cdot d\vec{S} = 0∬S​curl(F)⋅dS=0 for any closed SSS
  • If div⁡(F⃗)=0\operatorname{div}(\vec{F}) = 0div(F)=0 in EEE, the net outward flux through ∂E\partial E∂E is zero
  • Volume of EEE: V=13∬Sr⃗⋅n^ dS=13∬S⟨x,y,z⟩⋅dS⃗V = \frac{1}{3}\iint_S \vec{r}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = \frac{1}{3}\iint_S \langle x,y,z\rangle\cdot d\vec{S}V=31​∬S​r⋅n^dS=31​∬S​⟨x,y,z⟩⋅dS

Regions with Holes (Two Boundaries)

If DDD is a solid region with boundary S1S_1S1​ (outer) and S2S_2S2​ (inner), where S1S_1S1​ has normal outward and S2S_2S2​ has normal inward (into DDD):

∭Ddiv⁡(F⃗) dV=∬S1F⃗⋅dS⃗−∬S2F⃗⋅dS⃗\iiint_D \operatorname{div}(\vec{F})\,dV = \iint_{S_1}\vec{F}\cdot d\vec{S} - \iint_{S_2}\vec{F}\cdot d\vec{S}∭D​div(F)dV=∬S1​​F⋅dS−∬S2​​F⋅dS

Use this when a smaller solid (e.g., a sphere of radius r1r_1r1​) is removed from inside a larger one (e.g., a sphere of radius r2r_2r2​). The integral over the annular region equals the outer flux minus the inner flux.
